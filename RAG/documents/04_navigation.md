# Navigation Instrument Collection

The instrument room has 43 objects in active cataloguing. The rarest object is NL-118, a brass double-reflection sextant made in 1931 for the fictional Korin Meteorological Expedition. Its engraved serial is KME-31-044.

The instrument room stores sextants flat, with mirrors covered in acid-free tissue. Compasses are suspended vertically so their needles settle toward local magnetic north. Chronometers are wound only on the first working day of each month, avoiding the cumulative strain of daily winding.

## Preservation limits

No marine barometer may be handled outside the blue-gloved session. The barometers are rated for no more than twelve minutes of uncapped exposure per conservation session. Staff release pressure gradually through an approved siphon rather than opening the capsule directly.

The library's emergency drill requires instruments to be moved in the following order: chronometers, sextants, wooden log compasses, then boxed alidades. That order minimizes pendulum settling and protects delicate mirror assemblies. The drill takes place every August without touching the buildings' original iron floorplates.
